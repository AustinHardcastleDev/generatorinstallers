# GeneratorInstallerList Voice Profile

## Voice Summary
Practical research for people who need a whole-home generator installed by someone who’s actually done the job. We cut across shop types, filter the noise, and point you toward contractors who’ve signaled they do this work, so you can call, get a quote, and get it done. Brand-agnostic: Generac, Kohler, Cummins, or another line, the goal is the same.

## Core Personality Traits
- **Practical filter:** We exist to remove dead ends, not to curate a romance with a contractor. Help the reader call someone useful.
- **Category-clear:** Generator installs span electricians, HVAC shops, dedicated generator companies, and more. We say that mess out loud and sort for signal anyway.
- **Reliability-minded:** Outages and backup matter. Serious without panic theater or prepper cosplay.
- **Utilitarian guide:** Good work, fair price, someone who’s done this before. Less “interview on every axis,” more “get a capable installer out.”

## Tone Spectrum

| Dimension | Position | Notes |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| Formal ↔ Casual | Casual-professional | Plain speech, contractions OK, not slangy |
| Serious ↔ Playful | Mostly serious | Dry clarity when it helps; no jokey urgency |
| Reserved ↔ Bold | Clear, measured bold | Strong about the method; never “trusted pros” claims |
| Simple ↔ Sophisticated | Simple words, specific when needed | Licenses, ATS, fuel: plain first, precise second |
| Warm ↔ Direct | Direct first | Respect the reader’s time; no hand-holding fluff |

## Vocabulary

**Words/phrases to USE:**
- “done this before” / “someone who’s done this before”: core promise
- “filter the noise”: what the research is for
- “actually installs generators” / “show generator work”: signal standard
- “call and get someone out”: utilitarian next step
- electrician / HVAC / generator shop: name the category blur
- website signal, researched, independent: method language
- whole-home / standby: product language
- brand-agnostic framing: Generac, Kohler, Cummins, or another brand

**Words/phrases to AVOID:**
- “shortlist” / “cleaner shortlist” as the hero frame: house-building energy, wrong job here
- “dream team,” “trusted network,” “vetted pros,” “marketplace”
- interview-on-every-axis custom-home language
- panic / doom framing around outages
- AI filler: comprehensive, robust, seamless, elevate, unlock

**Punctuation rule:** Never use em dashes (—). Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead. Prefer clear sentence breaks over stacked asides.

**Jargon level:** Light to moderate. Translate trade terms when a buyer needs the concept.

**Profanity:** Never

## Rhythm & Structure

**Sentences:** Mix of short punchy lines and medium explainers. Rarely long. Prefer two clear sentences over one with a long mid-break.

**Paragraphs:** Short. 1–3 sentences. Guides can go longer when teaching a procedure.

**Openings:** Name the messy category or the utilitarian goal. Lead with the problem Maps doesn’t solve.

**Formatting:** Headers, lists, bold for the question the buyer should ask. Minimal emoji. No em dashes in body, titles, or CTAs.

## POV & Address

**First person:** We
**Reader address:** You, direct
**Relationship stance:** Guide who’s already done the sorting. Peer energy for experienced homeowners; not a guru, not a lead broker.

## Platform Adaptations

| Platform | Tone Shift | Structure | Length |
|----------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Email | Slightly warmer, still utilitarian | Short paragraphs, one clear CTA | 150–300 words |
| LinkedIn | Method and category insight first | Hook in line one, line breaks, one idea | 100–200 words |
| Twitter/X | Punchiest version of the filter | One idea, fragments OK | Under 280 chars |
| Blog/SEO | Same voice, more depth, still practical | Headers, checklists, sourced specifics | 1500–2500 words |
| Landing Page | Benefit + next call, brand-agnostic | Short sentences, clear CTA, proof of research | Hero tight; page varies |

## Example Phrases

**On-brand (sounds like us):**
- “Find a generator installer who’s done this before.”
- “Electricians, HVAC shops, generator companies: Maps doesn’t sort for who actually does standby work. We did.”
- “Generac, Kohler, Cummins, or another brand: start with installers who’ve signaled they do the work.”
- “Filter the noise. Call someone who can get it done.”

**Off-brand (doesn’t sound like us):**
- “Build your shortlist of trusted local pros.” Marketplace / Barndo-home energy.
- “We carefully vet every contractor in our premium network.” Fake verification.
- “When the grid fails, only the prepared survive.” Panic / prepper cosplay.
- “Unlock seamless whole-home backup with our comprehensive directory.” AI sludge.

## Do's and Don'ts

**DO:**
- Lead with the shop-type blur and the website-signal filter
- Emphasize “someone who’s done this before”
- Stay brand-agnostic: buyers can find installers for the brand they want
- Keep claims honest: research and signal, not endorsement or license checks
- Write for experienced, reliability-minded homeowners in outage-prone regions

**DON'T:**
- Use em dashes anywhere
- Center the brand on “shortlist” the way a custom-build directory would
- Imply we verify licenses, insurance, or workmanship
- Sound like a Generac (or any OEM) dealer finder
- Soften the utilitarian job into lifestyle content

# Choosing an Installer — What Separates a Good One From a Bad One

**Internal research only. Not published content.** This file exists so our writers can speak with grounded confidence about installer selection, licensing, and consumer protection. Nothing here may be presented to readers as legal advice. Licensing varies enormously by state and, in many states, by city or county — published copy must always tell the reader to verify against their own state board and local AHJ. Every state claim below was checked against that state's actual board, statute, or attorney general. Research date: August 2026.

---

## Executive summary

- **A home standby install is legally two trades in most states** — electrical contractor for the ATS and panel work, licensed plumber or gas fitter for the fuel line. Consumer Reports frames it the same way. ([CR](https://www.consumerreports.org/home-garden/generators/best-home-standby-generators-consumer-reports-tests-a5942327707/))
- **There is no national electrician license.** Six of the ten states we examined regulate electrical contracting statewide; four (NY, PA, OH for residential, MO by default) push it to cities and counties. New York has no state license at all — NYC DOB, Nassau *towns*, and Suffolk/Westchester/Dutchess county boards each issue their own, and none are reciprocal. ([NY County Law §236-a](https://statutes.nypti.org/$$CNT236-A$$))
- **In Massachusetts, gas fitting is a separate license from plumbing** — different board, different credential; a master plumber license does not authorize gas work. ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/orgs/board-of-state-examiners-of-plumbers-and-gas-fitters))
- **In Texas, three separate agencies** touch one install: TDLR (electrical), TSBPE (plumbing), Railroad Commission (LP-gas). ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/apply/businesses/contractor-elec.htm); [RRC](https://rrc.texas.gov/alternative-fuels/liquefied-petroleum-gas-propane/plumber-and-acr-contractor-registration/))
- **"Factory authorized" is a commercial program, not a competency license.** Kohler publishes a table showing a plain "Contractor" installs Kohler generators *non-certified*, cannot perform factory start-up, and cannot register the warranty. ([Kohler](https://assets.ctfassets.net/zlhl2y58oyef/7koKe6ScmFVinCjp3ZhKks/187004ec220ee1bbfcd9a738c7c094d2/g12416.pdf))
- **Generac does not require an authorized dealer to install** — it "strongly recommend[s]" it. Warranty *repairs* are different: work by anyone other than an Independent Authorized Service Dealer "will not be covered." ([support](https://support.generac.com/articles/Knowledge/How-do-I-activate-my-liquid-cooled-generator-with-a-Power-Zone-410-controller); [warranty](https://www.generac.com/globalassets/products/residential/standby-generators/warranty/5-year-limited-warranty-residential-commercial-standby-generators.pdf))
- **Warranty starts at activation, not purchase** — and Generac says units "should not be activated and registered until installation and all required permitting is complete." Activating before final inspection burns the customer's warranty clock. ([Generac](https://support.generac.com/articles/Knowledge/How-do-I-activate-my-liquid-cooled-generator-with-a-Power-Zone-410-controller))
- **Generac caps warranty travel at 100 miles / 3 hours round trip** from the nearest authorized dealer — a real limit on what "covered" means in rural markets.
- **A load calculation is a code obligation, not a courtesy.** NEC 702.4(B)(2) requires the standby source to supply the full automatically connected load per Article 220; 220.87 lets existing homes size off a year of utility demand data or a 30-day recorded 15-minute peak. ([EC&M](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/qa/article/20898908/code-qa-sizing-an-optional-standby-generator))
- **Gas sizing has a published pass/fail number:** pressure must not drop more than 2 in. w.c. from no-load to full-load and never below the nameplate minimum; a fluctuating manometer needle at full load means undersized or restricted piping. ([Generac design guide](https://legacy.genconnect.generac.com/Media/vwDoc.axd?d=41db23db-ec97-4907-8da3-844ec352fe1d))
- **Deposit law is the most concrete red-flag test.** California caps the down payment at **$1,000 or 10%, whichever is less**, with no special-order exception; Pennsylvania at **one-third** plus documented special-order materials. ([CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Home_Improvement_Contracts/What_Is_A_Contract.aspx); [73 P.S. §517.7](https://www.berkspa.gov/getmedia/01cb87e3-7f85-41b6-bd52-a763b5fd98e6/act_132_home_improvement.pdf))
- **Post-disaster contracting is a felony risk in Florida.** Unlicensed contracting is normally a first-degree misdemeanor; during a Governor-declared state of emergency it is a third-degree felony. ([Fla. Stat. 489.127(2)(c)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/489.127))
- **Storm-chasing enforcement is live and recent.** Missouri's AG charged one contractor with 23 felony counts over $131,885.91 from 16 consumers, most 2025 St. Louis tornado victims; a Pinellas County, FL sting after Helene/Milton produced ~40 arrests in two days. ([MO AG](https://ago.mo.gov/attorney-general-hanaway-announces-charges-against-contractor-targeting-st-louis-tornado-victims/); [FOX 13](https://www.fox13news.com/news/unlicensed-contractors-arrested-pinellas-county-during-aftermath-hurricanes-helene-milton))
- **Some states pay homeowners back when a licensed contractor fails** — Arizona up to $30,000 per residence, Maryland $30,000 per claimant, Massachusetts $25,000, but Massachusetts only if **the contractor, not the homeowner, pulled the permit**. ([AZ](https://roc.az.gov/recovery-fund); [MD](https://www.labor.md.gov/license/mhic/mhicfaqgf.shtml); [MA](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/file-a-guaranty-fund-claim-for-an-unpaid-hic-judgment))
- **The dominant BBB complaint theme is not bad installation — it's disappearing:** multi-month permit stalls after the deposit, and no-show service during the exact outage the generator was bought for.

## Detailed findings

### 1. Licensing: who is legally allowed to do which part

The work splits into three scopes, and almost every state treats them as different credentials: the ATS/panel/feeder work (electrical contractor license, state or local), the gas or LP piping and pressure test (plumber, gas fitter, or LP-gas installer), and the pad and trench (usually unregulated, or covered by a general/home-improvement registration).

Two things follow. **One company on the contract does not mean one trade doing the work** — the gas side is very often subcontracted, which is fine, but the homeowner should be told whose license covers it. And **the credential a company advertises may not be the credential that governs the risky part of the job**: a "licensed electrical contractor" badge says nothing about who sized and pressure-tested the fuel line.

#### State licensing comparison

| State | Electrical authority | Gas/fuel authority | Notes and thresholds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **California** (strict) | [CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/) — C-10 | CSLB — C-36 | License required at $500 labor+materials. AB 2622 (eff. 1/1/2025) raised the exemption to $1,000 **only where no permit is required** — which never applies here. |
| **Texas** (strict, three agencies) | [TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/apply/businesses/contractor-elec.htm) — must employ a Master Electrician of Record | [TSBPE](https://tsbpe.texas.gov/license-types/); [RRC](https://rrc.texas.gov/alternative-fuels/liquefied-petroleum-gas-propane/plumber-and-acr-contractor-registration/) for LP-gas | "Anyone who performs electrical work in the state of Texas must be licensed." $300k/occurrence, $600k aggregate insurance required. |
| **Florida** (strict, criminalized) | [DBPR](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/) ECLB | DBPR plumbing | Unlicensed contracting = 1st-degree misdemeanor; **3rd-degree felony during a Governor-declared emergency**. DBPR must refer cases to the State Attorney. |
| **Massachusetts** (unusual split) | [Board of State Examiners of Electricians](https://www.mass.gov/board-of-state-examiners-of-electricians-licensing) | [Plumbers and Gas Fitters](https://www.mass.gov/orgs/board-of-state-examiners-of-plumbers-and-gas-fitters) — **gas fitter separate from plumber** | Plus HIC registration, an arbitration program, and a $25,000 Guaranty Fund — forfeited if the homeowner pulled the permit. |
| **North Carolina** (tiered by value) | [NCBEEC](https://www.ncbeec.org/licensing/) | Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors | Ten classes. Limited ≤$60,000/project at ≤600V; Intermediate ≤$150,000; Unlimited unrestricted. Free public [lookup](https://arls-public.ncbeec.org/public/search). |
| **Louisiana** (low threshold) | [LSLBC](https://lslbc.louisiana.gov/faq/) — Electrical | LSLBC — Plumbing | Electrical/mechanical/plumbing license at **$10,000** project value vs. $50,000 for other specialties. Workers' comp certificate and $100,000 GL required at application and renewal. |
| **Ohio** (loose for residential) | [OCILB](https://com.ohio.gov/divisions-and-programs/industrial-compliance/boards/boards-overview) — **commercial only** | OCILB — commercial only | ORC Ch. 4740 excludes one-, two-, and three-family dwellings, so residential is governed locally; ORC 3781.102(B) lets counties license. Contracts over $25,000 need AG registration (ORC 4722). |
| **New York** (loosest) | **None statewide.** NYC DOB Master/Special; Nassau at the **town** level; Suffolk, Westchester, Dutchess county boards | Local | No reciprocity between jurisdictions. Homeowner permits are unavailable in NYC — a licensed electrician must file. |
| **Pennsylvania** (loose) | **None statewide.** Municipal only | Local | HICPA registration with the AG at $5,000/yr of residential work — **no exam, no experience proof, no CE**. Deposit capped at one-third plus special-order materials. |
| **Missouri** (opt-in) | [OSEC](https://pr.mo.gov/electricalcontractors.asp) statewide license is **optional**; subdivisions must accept it in lieu of a local one (RSMo 324.925) | No state board — local | No statewide GC, plumbing, or HVAC license exists. |

**Reading the table:** the honest generalization is that in most states the electrical side is regulated at some level and the fuel side is regulated separately — but the agency, the dollar threshold, and whether the credential is state or municipal change completely across state lines.

#### How a homeowner verifies a license number

1. **Get the number in writing on the proposal.** In California a license number must appear in advertising, and unlicensed persons cannot advertise for jobs over $1,000.
2. **Search the state board's own portal**, not an aggregator — [CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx), [MyFloridaLicense](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/), [NCBEEC](https://arls-public.ncbeec.org/public/search). For every other state, NASCLA's [CSLID](https://cslid.nascla.org/) identifies the 180+ agencies that regulate construction and answers "which board do I even call."
3. **Search by name *and* by number and confirm they match.** In California the number belongs to the qualifying individual and travels with them, so a number on a business card may belong to someone who left two years ago.
4. **Read every field, not just "active":** status, classification, bond, workers' comp, and complaint disclosure. CSLB shows all of them.
5. **Confirm the class fits the job.** North Carolina's Limited class caps a single project at $60,000; a mismatched class makes the work non-inspectable under board rules.

### 2. Factory-authorized dealer vs. independent electrician

This is the most over-claimed idea in the category, and the manufacturers' own documents are the best corrective. **Kohler publishes the distinction explicitly** in its Installer Program brochure:

| | Contractor | Installer | Direct Dealer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Installs Kohler generators | Yes (non-certified) | Yes (certified) | Yes (certified) |
| Provides factory start-up | No | Yes | Yes |
| Provides warranty registration | No | Yes | Yes |
| Provides service and warranty | No | No | Yes |
| Listed on Kohler's website | No | No | Yes |

Kohler "Installer" status means a free online Installer Fundamentals course (~16 hours, two-year certification) covering setbacks, fuel line requirements, pre-start checklists, ATS mounting, unlock codes, and start-up, plus a tool list including a manometer and a 20 kW load bank; an optional $800 two-day instructor-led course extends it to four years. Direct Dealers add annual purchase commitments, RCA/RCL-certified employees, and Salesforce/microsite participation — which is to say **the tier is substantially a commercial ranking** with a genuine but modest technical floor under it. ([Installer Program](https://assets.ctfassets.net/zlhl2y58oyef/7koKe6ScmFVinCjp3ZhKks/187004ec220ee1bbfcd9a738c7c094d2/g12416.pdf); [Direct Dealer Program](https://assets.ctfassets.net/zlhl2y58oyef/iw1N8xPSrOXnNdoc5dzNm/3268abc49c35debed7ed656465263cdb/g12441.pdf))

**Generac restructured its tiers in 2025** into Prestige, Premier, Elite Plus, Elite, and Select. The published differentiators are headcount and stock, not craftsmanship: Elite = one factory-trained technician plus essential maintenance parts; Elite Plus = two with liquid-cooled expertise and 24/7 emergency support; Premier = four; Select = "not every Select Dealer offers installation and service." ([Generac](https://www.generac.com/home-standby-generators/home-dealer-locator/dealer-tiers/))

**Authorization does guarantee** that somebody completed factory training and that the company can register the warranty, perform factory start-up, and get reimbursed for warranty repairs. **It does not guarantee** a state license (Kohler verifies none, and merely requires Installers to "provide customers with a certified service partner"), that the trained person is the one on your property, that the gas work is in-house, or that the manufacturer will make you whole if the installer fails.

**Warranty mechanics.** Generac's residential warranty begins "upon the successful start-up and/or on-line activation of the unit," requires registration, proof of purchase, and proof of all required maintenance, and excludes repairs or diagnostics by anyone other than an Independent Authorized Service Dealer. Travel is capped at 100 miles / 3 hours round trip. The lever is the *repair* restriction, not a maintenance restriction — Generac tech support has reportedly told owners self-performed maintenance is fine if done correctly and documented, but that is a forum relay and **anecdotal**. ([warranty](https://www.generac.com/globalassets/products/residential/standby-generators/warranty/5-year-limited-warranty-residential-commercial-standby-generators.pdf); [Gentek](https://gentekpower.com/forums/viewtopic.php?t=2101))

Cummins is blunter: units "must be installed by a certified electrician in compliance with all local building codes," and "if installation guidelines are not followed, the warranty will not apply." Base warranty is 5 years/2,000 hours on 13–20 kW and 2 years on 25–125 kW, extendable to 10 — and Cummins openly supports buying online and hiring your own installer. ([FAQ](https://www.cummins.com/na/generators/home-standby/home-generator-faq); [buying guide](https://www.cummins.com/rs-eu/mp-resource/en-na/na/generators/home-standby/buying-your-generator))

**Practical takeaway:** a non-authorized but properly licensed electrician is legal and often fine for the *installation*, but it leaves two gaps — someone still has to perform the authorized start-up for the warranty to begin, and every future warranty repair routes through a dealer who did not sell the unit and has no relationship with the customer. That second gap is where the horror stories come from.

### 3. What a complete quote contains

A quote that collapses everything into "Generator installed — $14,500" is not a quote; it's a number. A trustworthy proposal itemizes:

- **Unit** — brand, model, kW, fuel, ATS model and amperage, whether the ATS is service-entrance rated, whether load-shed modules are included.
- **The load calculation** — the method used (Article 220 calculation vs. 220.87 utility demand data) and the resulting calculated load. This is the line that determines whether the unit is correctly sized.
- **Gas** — pipe material, diameter, run length, who sized it, whether a step-down regulator or meter upgrade is needed, who coordinates with the utility or propane supplier.
- **Electrical** — conductor sizing, conduit/trench length, panel or subpanel work, grounding approach, any service upgrade.
- **Pad and site** — pad type, elevation for flood or snow, clearances confirmed against the manual.
- **Permits** — how many, which ones, and who files them.
- **Start-up** — who performs it, whether factory-authorized, and that activation happens *after* final inspection.
- **Cleanup and restoration**, including landscape, stucco, or siding repair.
- **Warranty** — equipment term stated separately from labor term.
- **Payment schedule** tied to milestones, with the deposit inside the state's legal cap.

Consumer Reports anchors why the itemization matters: units in its ratings run roughly $3,400–$7,000 while installation "can be more expensive than the unit itself, ranging from about $5,000 to $10,000 or more," and "smaller units cost almost as much to install as larger ones." When more than half the price is the install, an unitemized install line hides more than half the deal. ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/home-garden/generators/best-home-standby-generators-consumer-reports-tests-a5942327707/))

**A vague quote signals** a price generated from a phone call and a square-footage guess — meaning no load calculation, no site survey, no confirmed clearances, and no confirmed gas capacity. Those are exactly the four things that stall a project at permit or fail it at inspection.

### 4. Questions the buyer should ask

Publishable version in the checklist section below. The load-bearing ones: who pulls the permit, was a load calculation performed and by what method, how was the gas line sized and is a full-load manometer test planned, who performs factory start-up, who services it afterward and how fast during a regional outage, is any scope subcontracted, and what is the labor warranty as distinct from the equipment warranty.

### 5. Red flags

- **No license number on the written proposal**, or a number that does not match the board's record on both name and number search.
- **No permit, or "we don't need one for this."** Every home standby install involves an ATS and combustion appliance connection; a permit-free install is an inspection-free install. In Massachusetts, it also destroys Guaranty Fund eligibility.
- **A deposit above the state cap** — over $1,000 in California, over one-third in Pennsylvania. This is objectively verifiable and requires no judgment call from the buyer.
- **A quote without a site visit.** Clearances, gas capacity, panel condition, and trench route cannot be assessed remotely.
- **Door-to-door solicitation after a storm**, especially by an out-of-area crew. Wisconsin DATCP: storm chasers "travel throughout the Midwest during the spring and summer months," and "if something goes wrong, it could be impossible for a consumer to get their money back." ([DATCP](https://datcp.wi.gov/Pages/News_Media/20250429AfterStormDamageBeWaryOfDoorToDoorRepairCrews.aspx))
- **An offer to cover or discount the insurance deductible.** Illegal in Minnesota with fines up to $10,000 per violation, and prohibited under Wisconsin's storm-chaser law. ([Minn. Stat. 325E.66](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/325E.66); [MN DLI](https://dli.mn.gov/business/residential-contractors/contractors-and-insurance-deductibles))
- **Pressure to sign today**, price-expires tactics, or a refusal to leave a written proposal.
- **Cash-only or personal-account payment**, and no written contract.

### 6. Storm chasing and post-disaster fraud

Recent, citable enforcement:

- **Missouri, 2026.** AG Catherine Hanaway charged Jacob Kaestner (JK Exteriors) with 23 counts of deceptive business practices and financial exploitation of the elderly over 16 victims — most of them March and May 2025 St. Louis tornado survivors, seven elderly — with aggregate losses of $131,885.91. A second contractor, James Hampton Young (For Valor Roofing), was indicted on nine felony counts; the AG's office logged 76 complaints across the two cases. ([MO AG](https://ago.mo.gov/attorney-general-hanaway-announces-charges-against-contractor-targeting-st-louis-tornado-victims/); [reporting](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/missouri-ag-charges-two-contractors-213018637.html))
- **Florida, post-Helene/Milton.** A two-day Pinellas County undercover sting produced ~38–40 arrests on 44+ charges, run jointly by the Sheriff, Clearwater PD, county Consumer Protection, the county Licensing Board, DBPR, DFS, and the State Attorney. ([FOX 13](https://www.fox13news.com/news/unlicensed-contractors-arrested-pinellas-county-during-aftermath-hurricanes-helene-milton))
- **Florida, Dec 2025.** Bradenton PD arrested an unlicensed contractor on 13 felony charges, including **10 counts of presenting someone else's contractor's license during a declared state of emergency**, after 14 victims aged 62–95 paid $222,000 in down payments for Milton repairs never performed. ([WWSB](https://www.mysuncoast.com/2025/12/17/unlicensed-contractor-accused-defrauding-elderly-hurricane-milton-victims/))

**Deposit and cancellation protections (verified):**

- **Federal — FTC Cooling-Off Rule, 16 CFR Part 429.** Three business days to cancel a sale of $25 or more made at the buyer's home; Saturday counts, Sundays and federal holidays don't. The seller must give written and oral notice, and **the clock does not start until they do**. Excluded: sales negotiated at the seller's permanent place of business, and visits the buyer requested for repair or maintenance of existing property. ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buyers-remorse-ftcs-cooling-rule-may-help); [rule page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/cooling-period-sales-made-home-or-other-locations))
- **California.** Down payment ≤ $1,000 or 10%, whichever is less, **no exception for special-order materials**; progress payments may not exceed the value of work performed (B&P §§7159, 7159.5). ([CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Home_Improvement_Contracts/What_Is_A_Contract.aspx))
- **Pennsylvania.** Deposit ≤ one-third of contract price plus documented special-order materials; contracts over $500 must be written and carry the HIC number; contracts over $5,000 carry a three-day rescission notice. ([73 P.S. §517.7](https://www.berkspa.gov/getmedia/01cb87e3-7f85-41b6-bd52-a763b5fd98e6/act_132_home_improvement.pdf))
- **Minnesota.** No paying or offering to pay any part of a deductible, and no yard-sign allowances, free upgrades, meals, or "anything of value" as inducement; fines to $10,000 per violation. Minn. Stat. 326B.811 separately gives a **72-hour right to cancel** after the insurer denies a claim. ([MN DLI](https://dli.mn.gov/workers/homeowners/hiring-contractor-storm-repair))
- **Wisconsin.** No deductible payments, no negotiating with the insurer without permission, must ask whether work is insurance-related before contracting, and must give notice of a **three-business-day right to cancel if the insurer denies any portion of the claim**. ([DATCP](https://datcp.wi.gov/Pages/News_Media/20250429AfterStormDamageBeWaryOfDoorToDoorRepairCrews.aspx))

### 7. Insurance, bonding, and recovery funds

**Why it matters concretely:** if the crew damages the house, the general liability policy pays; if a worker is injured on the property and there is no workers' comp, the recovery route runs toward the property owner. Homeowners' policies generally do not cover contractor negligence.

Some states build minimums into licensure, which gives us hard numbers: Texas requires $300,000 per occurrence, $600,000 aggregate, and $300,000 products/completed operations for an electrical contractor license. Louisiana requires a workers' comp certificate and $100,000 general liability at application and renewal. Pennsylvania's HICPA registration requires only $50,000 personal injury and $50,000 property damage — worth flagging in copy as registration-level, not project-level.

**Verification, in order of reliability:** check the state board record first where it tracks coverage (**CSLB shows bond and workers' comp status directly**, including the exemption reason when there is none); ask for an ACORD 25 certificate sent by the insurer; then **call the carrier at a number found independently, not the one printed on the certificate** — forged or lapsed certificates are the known failure mode; finally, confirm the dates span the whole project and the policy doesn't exclude the trade.

**Recovery funds** are the underrated backstop and deserve their own guide section, because they pay out only against **licensed** contractors — which is itself the strongest argument for hiring one:

| State | Fund | Per-claim cap | Per-contractor cap | Notable condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arizona | Residential Contractors' Recovery Fund | $30,000 per residence | $200,000 per license, prorated | Requires a final court or administrative order; 2-year limitations period |
| Maryland | MHIC Guaranty Fund | $30,000 per claimant (or amount paid, whichever is less) | $250,000, prorated | 3 years from discovery |
| Massachusetts | HIC Guaranty Fund | $25,000 actual loss | — | **The contractor, not the homeowner, must have pulled the permit**; requires an unpaid judgment/award and exhausted collection |

### 8. Service after the sale

Generators are unusual among home equipment in that **the product's entire value is realized in a window when every servicing company in the region is simultaneously overwhelmed.** A dishwasher that fails on a Tuesday can wait a week. A generator that fails four hours into a hurricane outage has failed completely.

Four structural reasons the servicing relationship matters more here: warranty repair is channel-restricted to authorized dealers, with Generac capping warranty travel at 100 miles / 3 hours round trip; manufacturers formalize response capability into tiers (Generac's Elite Plus is defined partly by "24/7 customer support for emergencies," Prestige by "fast response times," and Kohler's Direct Dealers must respond to leads within 24 hours); parts stock is itself a tier criterion, with Elite dealers keeping "essential maintenance parts" and Elite Plus a "broader inventory"; and the unit needs ongoing exercise, oil, filters, and valve-lash checks, with Generac's warranty conditioned on proof of required maintenance being available.

The buyer-facing version: *ask what happened during the last regional outage* — how many units under contract, how many technicians, what the actual median response time was. A company that cannot answer has not measured it.

### 9. Recurring consumer complaint themes

Sources here are BBB complaint records (real filed complaints, but one-sided) and homeowner/contractor forums (**anecdotal**).

**Permit stalls after the deposit is taken** is the single most common BBB pattern. A Florida homeowner reported four months in with the install one-third done and permitting blocked by a setback problem "they neglected to consider when they did the site survey" — learned only by going to the building department personally; another reported six months to permit approval. A Texas customer quoted five weeks was at double that with no pad and no generator. ([BBB — Generx](https://www.bbb.org/us/fl/oldsmar/profile/electric-generator/generx-generators-inc-0653-90082906/customer-reviews); [BBB — Grasten](https://www.bbb.org/us/tx/conroe/profile/generator/grasten-power-technologies-llc-0915-90006392/complaints))

**No-show service during the outage the unit was bought for.** A Texas homeowner with an active $500 service contract reported the generator running four hours during Hurricane Beryl then stopping, 2.5 days without power, and 20+ unreturned messages. A Louisiana homeowner logged three separate calls across a month of post-outage waiting. ([BBB — Generator Industries](https://www.bbb.org/us/tx/pearland/profile/generator/generator-industries-0915-90038588/complaints); [BBB — Generac](https://www.bbb.org/us/wi/waukesha/profile/electric-generator/generac-power-systems-inc-0694-15012025/complaints?page=22&type=serviceorrepair))

**Gas supply problems that surface only under full load.** Generac's manual predicts this exactly: "a drop in gas pressure indicates that the supply is barely adequate," and "a fluctuating needle indicates that gas piping may be undersized or restricted." A forum thread (**anecdotal**) shows the textbook case — 3/4" flex run 60 ft where the manual called for 1-1/4", steady at partial load, needle jumping wildly once both AC units ran. InspectAPedia (secondary trade reference) calls undersized supply piping among the most common LP install errors. ([Generac manual](https://www.electricgeneratorsdirect.com/manuals/Generac%20Install%20Manual%208-20kW-rev-6-29-2011.pdf); [Gentek forum](https://gentekpower.com/forums/viewtopic.php?t=1497); [InspectAPedia](https://inspectapedia.com/electric/Backup-Electrical-Generator-Gas-Piping.php))

**Abandonment after payment** and **unfinished restoration** round out the set — one homeowner reported paying $17,500 in July, being promised a refund in December, and hearing nothing after (**anecdotal**); others report chipped stucco never repaired, enclosure doors never installed, enclosures that don't drain. ([AskALawyerOnCall](https://www.askalawyeroncall.com/law/lb0kb-need-guidance-paid-company-ohio-install.html))

Notably, **"the generator was undersized" appears far less often in BBB records than "nobody called me back."** Our content should reflect that distribution: sizing errors are a real technical risk, but process failure — permits, communication, service response — is what actually generates complaints.

## Claims we can make

**Write these principle-first.** Where a claim carries a dated figure — a price, a year's outage
data, a code edition — lead with the durable point and let the number follow as support. A sentence
built that way degrades into "roughly" rather than into "false." Always carry the year.

The durable material here is **how to evaluate an installer** — the trades involved, what
"authorized" does and does not mean, what belongs in a quote, which red flags are statutory. State
licensing thresholds and deposit limits change by legislature and should carry their state and a
"verify current" hedge.

1. Installing a home standby generator is normally two licensed trades, not one: an electrical contractor for the transfer switch and panel work, and a licensed plumber or gas fitter for the fuel line. — https://www.consumerreports.org/home-garden/generators/best-home-standby-generators-consumer-reports-tests-a5942327707/
2. There is no national electrician's license, and four of the ten states we examined — New York, Pennsylvania, Missouri by default, and Ohio for residential work — leave residential electrical licensing to cities and counties rather than the state. — https://statutes.nypti.org/$$CNT236-A$$ and https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3781.102
3. In Massachusetts, gas fitting is licensed separately from plumbing by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, and a master plumber license does not by itself authorize gas fitting work. — https://www.mass.gov/orgs/board-of-state-examiners-of-plumbers-and-gas-fitters
4. In Texas, an electrical contractor license requires the company to employ a Master Electrician of Record and to carry at least $300,000 per occurrence and $600,000 aggregate in liability insurance. — https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/apply/businesses/contractor-elec.htm

6. In North Carolina, electrical contractor licenses are tiered by project value: Limited is capped at $60,000 per project at 600 volts or less, Intermediate at $150,000, and Unlimited has no cap. — https://www.ncbeec.org/licensing/
7. In Florida, unlicensed contracting is a first-degree misdemeanor, but it becomes a third-degree felony when committed during a state of emergency declared by the Governor. — https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/489.127

## Buyer question checklist

*(Ready to publish. Each question is followed by what a good answer sounds like.)*

1. **What is your license number, and which board issued it?** — A specific number and a specific board, printed on the proposal. "We're licensed and insured" with no number is not an answer.
2. **Who is doing the gas work — your own licensed gas fitter, or a subcontractor?** — A named company or in-house license, with that number available too.
3. **Who pulls the permits, and how many will this job need?** — "We do, and it's typically an electrical and a gas permit, sometimes a building or zoning permit for the pad." If they say no permit is needed, walk.
4. **Did you perform a load calculation, and can I see it?** — A written calculation citing Article 220, or utility demand data used under 220.87. Not "a 22 kW handles most houses this size."
5. **How was the gas line sized, and will you do a full-load manometer test at start-up?** — A stated pipe diameter and run length matched to the manual's chart, and a yes on the test with the target pressure drop named.
6. **Who performs the factory start-up, and when relative to final inspection?** — A factory-certified person, *after* the final inspection passes, because that's when the warranty clock starts.
7. **Who services this afterward, and what were your response times in the last regional outage?** — A real technician count and a real response window. "We'll take care of you" is not a number.
8. **Are you a factory-authorized dealer for this brand, and at what tier?** — A named tier they're willing to have you verify on the manufacturer's dealer locator.
9. **Is any part of this job subcontracted?** — A straight yes or no, with names. Subcontracting is normal; concealing it is not.
10. **What's the warranty on your labor, separately from the equipment warranty?** — A stated labor term in writing, distinct from the manufacturer's equipment term.
11. **What is the deposit, and what is the payment schedule tied to?** — A deposit within your state's legal cap, with payments tied to completed milestones rather than dates.
12. **Can your insurer send me a certificate of insurance directly?** — Yes, without friction: general liability and workers' comp, dates covering the whole project.
13. **What's included for site restoration and cleanup?** — Specific language on trench backfill, sod, stucco or siding repair, and debris removal.
14. **What does your maintenance plan cover, what does it cost, and does it move me up the queue during an outage?** — A written scope, an annual price, and an honest answer on priority, including that nobody can guarantee same-day service in a regional event.
15. **Can you give me three local references from the last year, including one who has had a service call?** — Provided without delay.

## Claims to avoid

- **"You must use a factory-authorized dealer."** Generac "strongly recommends" it, Kohler runs a tiered program, Cummins requires a certified electrician and code compliance. None requires dealer authorization to install. Say "warranty *repairs* must go through an authorized dealer" instead — that one is documented.
- **"Using a non-authorized installer voids your warranty."** Overbroad. The documented restrictions cover repairs and diagnostics, activation/registration, and following the installation guidelines. Cummins comes closest ("if installation guidelines are not followed, the warranty will not apply") — a compliance condition, not an authorization condition.
- **"Doing your own maintenance voids your Generac warranty."** The written warranty conditions on proof of maintenance being available, not on who performed it. Our only contrary-direction source is a forum relay of a phone call — anecdotal, not citable.
- **"Every state requires a licensed electrical contractor."** False in this form: Ohio's state license excludes one-to-three-family dwellings, New York and Pennsylvania have none, Missouri's is optional.
- **"An unpermitted install voids your homeowners insurance."** No source. Use the documented consequences instead — resale disclosure, no inspection, and loss of Guaranty Fund eligibility in Massachusetts.
- **"A licensed contractor is a good contractor."** Licensing is a floor; Pennsylvania's HICPA registration requires no exam, no experience proof, and no CE.
- **Naming specific companies from BBB complaints.** Use the patterns, not the names.
- **Any "typical installed cost" figure presented as fact.** Published ranges disagree wildly ($7,000–$23,000). Consumer Reports' framing — installation often exceeds the unit price, and small units cost nearly as much to install as large ones — is the defensible version.
- **State licensing tables generated without checking each board.** The variation is too large to infer.

# Home Standby Generator Costs and Pricing — Internal Research

**Internal research only. Not published content.** This is a working reference for writers and editors at GeneratorInstallerList.com. Every figure below is labeled by source type (MSRP / retailer / manufacturer guidance / vendor estimate / unverified). Do not publish any figure from this document without carrying its source label and citation into the published copy. Research compiled August 7, 2026.

---

## Executive summary

- **The single most important clarification we can publish: equipment MSRP is roughly 40–55% of the finished project.** A Generac 22kW Guardian with a whole-house transfer switch carries a $6,979 MSRP ([Generac](https://www.generac.com/residential-products/standby-generators/gaseous/22kw-standby-generator-with-whole-house-switch-wifi-enabled-7043/)), while Generac's own installed-cost guidance for a complete project is **$8,000–$16,000** ([Generac answer on Home Depot product Q&A](https://www.homedepot.com/p/Generac-26-000-Watt-Dual-Fuel-Air-Cooled-Whole-House-Home-Standby-Generator-Smart-Home-Monitoring-7290/320593416)).
- **Verified 2026 air-cooled MSRPs (manufacturer sites):** Generac 10kW $3,649; 18kW $5,379 (unit only) / $6,229 (with whole-house switch); 22kW $6,119 / $6,979; 24kW $7,399; 26kW $6,989 / $7,899. Kohler 10kW $3,834; 14kW $4,997; 20kW $5,813; 26kW $7,445.
- **Liquid-cooled is a different price class entirely.** Kohler 24RCLA (24kW liquid-cooled) MSRP is **$17,900** — more than double the 26kW air-cooled Kohler at $7,445. Generac's 32kW XG Protector MSRP is $21,099; 48kW is $25,179.
- **Liquid-cooled street pricing diverges sharply from MSRP.** Electric Generators Direct lists the Generac XG 32kW single-phase at **$15,697** against a $21,099 MSRP. Always quote street price and MSRP separately for liquid-cooled.
- **Consumer Reports' independent framing is the best third-party anchor we have:** units in their ratings run **$3,400 to nearly $7,000**, and installation runs **about $5,000 to $10,000 or more** — often exceeding the unit cost ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/home-garden/generators/best-home-standby-generators-consumer-reports-tests-a5942327707/)).
- **Consumer Reports also makes a sizing-economics point worth repeating:** smaller units cost almost as much to install as larger ones, so the marginal cost of stepping up in kW is small relative to the total project.
- **Component prices we can cite from retailers:** 200A smart ATS $699–$1,299; 3" composite pad $399.99 (4" hurricane-rated $479); Generac 20–22kW maintenance kit $29.99 at Home Depot.
- **Extended warranty pricing comes straight from Generac's dealer program:** 5-year $535, 7-year $735, 10-year $1,035 on air-cooled; 7-year $1,065 and 10-year $1,485 on 22–60kW liquid-cooled.
- **Remote monitoring is mostly free.** Generac Mobile Link Basic monitoring is $0 for up to two generators; Premium is $5.49/month or $54.99/year via web. This contradicts a lot of "subscription trap" chatter.
- **Financing norms are well documented by Generac itself:** an 18-month deferred-interest plan (fixed payment = 2.50% of highest promo balance) and a **9.99% APR fixed for 132 months** (fixed payment = 1.25% of balance), both with a $29 activation fee, on the Synchrony Project Card. On a $13,000 project that's roughly $325/month for 18 months or $163/month for 132 months.
- **The best resale evidence we have is Zonda's 2025 Cost vs. Value Report,** which added "Backup Power Generator" to its project list for the first time: national job cost $13,534, resale value $12,902, **95.3% cost recouped**, ranking 7th of 28 projects.
- **We cannot trace the ubiquitous "adds 3–5% to home value / Consumer Reports says so" claim to any Consumer Reports publication.** Dozens of contractor blogs assert it; none link to a primary source. Treat as unverified marketing folklore and do not repeat it.
- **Ongoing ownership cost is modest:** Angi's 2026 data puts a maintenance visit at **$171–$396 (average $274)** and annual upkeep at **$200–$600/year**.
- **The "cost of not having one" is best argued with outage duration, not dollar estimates.** EIA reports US customers averaged **11 hours** without power in 2024 — nearly double the prior decade's average — with hurricanes causing 80% of those hours.

### 4. What drives a quote to the high end

Ranked by how much they move a number, based on the component pricing above:

1. **Electrical service or panel upgrade.** A home on 100A or 150A service needing a 200A upgrade adds a full sub-project. HomeGuide's separate panel-upgrade data puts this at several thousand dollars; we have not verified a generator-specific figure. Flag as the biggest single swing factor and the most common reason two quotes on the same unit differ by $4,000.
2. **Long gas runs.** At $12–$25 per foot installed (HomeGuide vendor estimate), a 60-foot run from meter to pad is $720–$1,500 in gas work alone before trenching restoration.
3. **Buried propane tank instead of a natural gas tie-in.** Underground 500-gallon installs run $1,500–$5,000 versus a natural gas connection. This is often the difference between a $12,000 and a $16,000 project.
4. **Difficult access.** Rock, high water table, tree roots, tight side yards, and back-yard placements requiring equipment all raise excavation and placement labor. Not separately priced in any source we verified.
5. **Coastal and high-wind requirements.** The verified cost signal here is small at the pad level — a 4" hurricane-rated GenPad is $479 versus $399.99 standard, an $80 delta. The larger coastal costs are anchoring, corrosion-resistant hardware, and elevation requirements in flood zones, which we did **not** verify with pricing. Kohler markets its enclosures as tested to 181 mph winds (manufacturer claim, [Kohler](https://www.kohlerhomeenergy.rehlko.com/products/home-generators)).
6. **Gas meter upsizing.** A 22kW Generac consumes 327 ft³/hr of natural gas at 100% load ([Generac 22kW spec](https://www.generac.com/residential-products/standby-generators/gaseous/22kW-Standby-Generator-WiFi-Enabled-7042/)), which can exceed an older residential meter's capacity. Cost is utility-dependent and **unverified**; the schedule risk (weeks of utility lead time) may matter more than the fee.
7. **Service relocation.** Not verified. Do not put a number on it.

### 5. Regional cost variation

Verified regional signals, in descending order of confidence:

- **Resale value varies more than cost does.** Zonda's 2025 CVV regional data shows Backup Power Generator at **96.5% cost recouped in the South Atlantic** (job cost $13,561, resale $13,089) versus **90.5% in the East North Central** (job cost $12,877, resale $11,651). Zonda notes ROI "exceeding 100% in hurricane-prone and storm-affected regions" and that Pacific and West South Central posted the strongest overall returns. Note that the *job cost* spread between those two regions is only about 5%; the *value* spread is what moves.
- **Labor rates.** Electricians bill $50–$130/hour nationally (HomeGuide, Angi — vendor estimates), with high-cost metros at the top of that band. We could not source a generator-specific, state-by-state labor table from an authoritative publisher. State-level "generator cost by state" tables circulating online are modeled from national averages, not collected data — **do not cite them.**
- **Permit fees are hyper-local and small in absolute terms.** Galveston, TX publishes $150 building + $25 plumbing + $20 electrical = **$195 total** for a residential backup power install. Houston requires separate single-trade electrical and plumbing permits, each pulled by the respective licensed contractor. National vendor estimates put the permit stack at $80–$450. Permits are almost never the reason a quote is high.
- **Coastal requirements** add hurricane-rated pads and anchoring but the verified pad delta is only about $80. Treat "coastal costs a lot more" as directionally true but poorly quantified in our sources.

Honest framing for published copy: **regional variation in installed price is real but is driven mainly by labor markets and site conditions, not by permit fees or equipment prices** (equipment MSRP is national).

### 6. Financing

**Dealer financing (Generac's own published disclosures — highest confidence)**

Generac partners with Synchrony on the Synchrony Project Card / Generac Credit Card. Two promotional structures are published:

| Plan | Terms | Fixed monthly payment | Fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18-month deferred interest | No interest if the promo balance is paid in full within 18 months; otherwise interest accrues **from the purchase date** | 2.50% of highest promo balance | $29 activation |
| 132-month reduced APR | **9.99% APR**, interest charged from purchase date, fixed payments until paid in full | 1.25% of highest promo balance | $29 activation |

Non-promotional terms for new accounts as of 8/29/25: **Purchase APR 26.99%**, minimum interest charge $2. Source: [Generac financing disclosures](https://www.generac.com/home-standby-generators/financing/disclosures/) and [Generac financing page](https://www.generac.com/home-standby-generators/financing/).

**Worked monthly payment examples** (arithmetic from the published payment formulas, on a $13,000 installed project):
- 18-month plan: 2.50% × $13,000 = **$325/month**
- 132-month plan at 9.99%: 1.25% × $13,000 = **$163/month** (rounded up to the next whole dollar per the disclosure)

**The deferred-interest trap is a real consumer-protection point worth publishing.** Under the 18-month plan, if the balance is not paid in full by month 18, interest is charged retroactively from the purchase date at the standard purchase APR. Minimum payments are explicitly disclosed as possibly insufficient to pay off the promo balance before the promo period ends.

**Tax credits and incentives are out of scope for this library.** They were researched and removed
because they turn on legislation and funding cycles we cannot track (see
`09-alternatives-battery-solar-portable.md`). Make no incentive claim of any kind; if a page must
address it, send readers to [DSIRE](https://www.dsireusa.org/) and their tax advisor.

**Insurance discounts are also unsupported.** Contractor blogs claim insurers offer 3–10% premium
discounts for a standby generator; we found no insurer documentation. Do not publish that claim.

### 8. Home resale value

**The one real study: Zonda 2025 Cost vs. Value Report (38th annual, published September 18, 2025).**

"Backup Power Generator" appeared in the report's project list for the first time in 2025 and entered the national top 10 at #7:

| Metric | National | South Atlantic | East North Central |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job cost | $13,534 | $13,561 | $12,877 |
| Resale value | $12,902 | $13,089 | $11,651 |
| Cost recouped | **95.3%** | **96.5%** | **90.5%** |

Sources: [Zonda](https://zondahome.com/2025-cost-vs-value-report/), [Zonda press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/zondas-38th-annual-cost-vs-value-report-confirms-exterior-projects-continue-to-deliver-the-highest-roi-302559084.html), [JLC South Atlantic regional page](https://www.jlconline.com/cost-vs-value/2025/south-atlantic/).

Methodology caveat we should disclose when citing it: CVV resale values come from **surveys of real estate professionals**, paired with modeled construction costs from Verisk's XactRemodel. It is an opinion-of-value survey, not a paired-sales regression. It is still by a wide margin the most credible generator resale figure in existence.

Zonda's own highlight notes generator ROI "exceeding 100% in hurricane-prone and storm-affected regions." A secondary summary reports the regional spread ran from roughly 139% at the high end to 69% at the low end; we did not verify those two endpoint regions directly and should not publish those numbers until we do.

**The appraiser's view (qualitative, credible, and a useful counterweight).** A practicing residential appraisal firm writes that appraisers treat generators as "a feature or amenity, not a dollar-for-dollar value increase," that value support depends on whether comparable sales in the market also included generators, and that in many cases "a generator may help a home sell more easily rather than sell for more" ([Town & Country Residential Appraisals, May 2026](http://www.tncresappraisals.com/blog/2026/5/25/do-whole-house-backup-generators-add-value-what-appraisers-actually-see)). This is a single firm's blog, not research — cite it as a practitioner perspective, not as data.

**The "3–5%" claim: we cannot trace it.** At least half a dozen contractor and realtor blogs assert that "Consumer Reports states a properly installed whole-home generator can increase a home's value by 3% to 5%." None of them link to a Consumer Reports article. We reviewed Consumer Reports' own home standby generator ratings page and generator buying guide and found **no such statement** — CR discusses purchase and installation cost and sizing economics, not resale percentages. Similarly, the "150% ROI from a Remodeling Magazine appraiser survey" claim appears only in contractor marketing with no citation, and it contradicts Zonda's actual 95.3% figure. **Both claims should be treated as unverified marketing folklore.**

### 9. The honest cost of not having one

Only figures we can source. Note that none of these are "the generator pays for itself" arguments — they are risk-framing.

**Outage exposure (US EIA, primary source)**
- US electricity customers averaged **11 hours** of interruptions in 2024, nearly twice the prior decade's annual average. Major events — chiefly Hurricanes Beryl, Helene, and Milton — accounted for **80%** of those hours. Customers averaged **1.5 interruptions** each. Source: [EIA Today in Energy](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=66744).
- Excluding major events, non-event interruptions "routinely average about two hours per year."
- State spread in 2024 was extreme: South Carolina averaged about 53 hours, Maine about 30, Florida/North Carolina/Texas roughly 25–30, while Arizona, South Dakota, North Dakota, and Massachusetts each averaged under 2 hours. This is the single best argument for **why the value of a generator is location-dependent** — and it is fully sourced.
- Comparison point: US customers averaged **5.6 hours** in 2022 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=61303)).

**Food loss (FDA / USDA FSIS / CDC, primary sources)**
- A refrigerator keeps food safe for **about 4 hours** unopened. A **full freezer holds temperature ~48 hours; half-full ~24 hours.** Perishables held above 40°F for 2 hours or more must be discarded. Sources: [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-during-emergencies/power-outages-key-tips-consumers-about-food-safety), [USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/emergencies/keep-your-food-safe-during-emergencies), [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/keep-food-safe-after-emergency.html).
- **We have no authoritative dollar figure for the cost of restocking a refrigerator and freezer.** Publish the 4/48/24-hour thresholds, not a dollar estimate.

**Frozen pipes and water damage (insurer data)**
- State Farm: from 2024 through June 2025, more than **20,000 claims** for frozen pipe and winter water damage totaling over **$628 million** in paid losses, with an **average claim payment exceeding $30,000** ([State Farm newsroom](https://newsroom.statefarm.com/frozen-pipes-not-so-nice-beat-winter-water-woes-before-they-strike/)).
- State Farm, earlier period (Aug 2022–Aug 2023): **$806 million** paid nationwide, **average claim $25,000** ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2024/01/18/756137.htm)).
- Guidewire HazardHub: average frozen pipe claim **approximately $18,000**; 21% of US homes face frozen pipe risk; per the Insurance Information Institute, water damage and freezing account for nearly 24% of all homeowners claims ([Guidewire](https://guidewire.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/hazardhub-winter-weather-data-helps-insurers-and-homeowners)).
- Note the spread: $18,000 to $30,000+ depending on insurer and period. Cite a range and name the source, never a single "average."
- **Critical honesty caveat:** these are frozen-pipe claims from all causes, overwhelmingly from unheated or under-heated structures during cold snaps. They are **not** "damage caused by power outages." A generator reduces one pathway to this outcome. Do not imply the full average claim is avoided cost.

**Hotel stays**
- The US hotel industry's average daily rate was **$160.54 in 2025** (CoStar/STR data as summarized in industry reporting; AHLA's own 2026 State of the Industry report is paywalled at $349). Treat the ADR as a rough per-night proxy only. We have no data on how often outage-displaced homeowners actually book hotels.

**Sump pump failure:** we found no sourceable figure isolating outage-caused sump pump failure losses. Do not publish one.

## Claims to avoid

- **"A whole-house generator adds 3–5% to your home's value."** Untraceable. Repeated across dozens of contractor and realtor blogs, always attributed to "Consumer Reports," never with a link. We reviewed Consumer Reports' generator ratings and buying guide and found no such statement. It also conflicts with the one real dataset (Zonda: 95.3% of cost recouped — i.e., a slight net loss, not a percentage-of-home-value gain). **Do not publish under any framing.**
- **"Up to 150% ROI, per a Remodeling Magazine appraiser survey."** No such survey located. Remodeling's Cost vs. Value franchise is now published by Zonda and its 2025 generator figure is 95.3%, not 150%.
- **"A generator pays for itself."** Nothing in our sources supports this. The Zonda data shows recovery of about 95% of cost at resale in a strong region — a near-wash, not a profit. Avoid "investment" and "pays for itself" language entirely.
- **"Homeowners insurance discounts of 3–10% for having a generator."** Asserted by contractor blogs; no insurer documentation found. Unverified.
- **Any per-square-foot pricing.** Our guides use installed system price ranges by kW. There is no credible $/sq ft basis for generator work and none of our sources publish one.
- **Angi's "$1,519–$8,813 normal range" as a whole-house standby installed price.** That range spans portables and partial systems. Citing it as a standby install cost would understate reality by thousands.
- **State-by-state "generator installation cost" tables** found on aggregator sites. Every one we examined was modeled by applying a labor multiplier to a national average, not collected from actual quotes. They look authoritative and are not.
- **Averaged frozen-pipe claim figures presented as outage-avoided cost.** The $18,000–$30,000+ claim averages are for all frozen-pipe causes, not power-outage-caused freezes. Cite them only as context for what a cold-weather failure can cost, never as "what a generator saves you."
- **Restocking-the-fridge dollar estimates.** Widely quoted ($300–$500 is common online) with no traceable source. Use the FDA/USDA time thresholds instead.
- **Liquid-cooled MSRP quoted alone.** Street pricing on the Generac XG 32kW runs about $5,400 below MSRP. Quoting MSRP only would materially mislead.

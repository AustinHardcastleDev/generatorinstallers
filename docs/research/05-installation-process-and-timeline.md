# Home Standby Generator Installation: Process and Timeline (Research Brief)

**Internal research only — not published content.** This is source material for writers building FAQ sections and buyer guides on GeneratorInstallerList.com. Every claim below carries its source. Do not publish this document as-is.

Research date: August 7, 2026. Scope: US residential home standby generators (air-cooled, 8–28 kW, natural gas or LP).

---

## Executive summary

- **End-to-end is 4–8 weeks; the physical install is 1–2 days.** Installers converge on 4–6 weeks ([Courtesy Electric](https://www.courtesyelectric.com/blog/generator-installation-what-to-expect)) to 4–8 weeks ([Malfettone Electric, NJ](https://malfettoneelectric.com/blog/generator-installation-timeline-nj)). Generac's own consumer page: installation "usually takes hours, but the process of preparing for it takes multiple weeks" ([Generac](https://www.generac.com/resources/home-management/backup-generator-installation/)).
- **Equipment lead time is rarely the bottleneck — permits, the gas utility, and installer labor are.** Air-cooled home standby is not backordered the way it was in 2021–2022, and manufacturers added plant automation specifically so that storm surges stop producing factory backlogs. Check current conditions locally rather than assuming a national wait.
- **The most under-discussed delay is a gas meter or service upgrade.** Only the utility can do it, adding 2–4 weeks ([Consumers Energy](https://101generator.com/consumers-energy-gas-meter-upgrade-generator-installation/)) to 4–8 weeks ([PSE&G/JCP&L](https://malfettoneelectric.com/blog/generator-installation-timeline-nj)). A 22 kW unit adds ~300,000 BTU/hr against meters typically rated 250,000–400,000 BTU/hr ([CenterPoint context](https://www.rootermantx.com/blog/gas-line-installation-whole-home-generator-hurricane-season/)).
- **Clearances are manufacturer requirements.** Generac: 3 ft front and ends, 18 in rear, 5 ft overhead ([instruction sheet](https://productmanuals.generac.com/api/manualfiles/G0072580/A0005151099/0)). The 18-in rear figure is an NFPA 37 §4.1.4 exception validated by Southwest Research Institute fire testing — the default is 5 ft, and the exception does not apply to openings.
- **Generac warns that CO can still enter a house at code-compliant spacing:** a unit placed per NFPA "may still allow CO within the structure. Unit may need to be installed farther from the structure than the NFPA requires" ([Generac](https://productmanuals.generac.com/api/manualfiles/G0072580/A0005151099/0)).
- **Undersized gas pipe is the most frequently cited install error.** Generac names it: "Gas pipe sizing is one of the most commonly made mistakes... The generator inlet size has no bearing on the proper gas pipe size" ([Generac 8–22 kW guidelines](https://inspectapedia.com/electric/Generac-Electric-Generator-IO-Manual.pdf)).
- **The tell is pressure under load, not at rest.** Generac's commissioning steps require recording gas pressure at full load; "a fluctuating needle indicates that gas piping may be undersized or restricted" ([9–22 kW manual](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/Generac_9kw-22kw_Installation_Manual.pdf)). Spec: 3.5–7 in WC natural gas, 10–12 in WC LP vapor.
- **Warranty starts at startup/activation — but "factory-authorized startup" is softer than marketing implies.** Generac's terms: warranty "begins upon the successful start-up and/or on-line activation," the unit must be registered, and repairs must go through an Independent Authorized Service Dealer ([warranty PDF](https://www.generac.com/globalassets/products/residential/standby-generators/warranty/5-year-limited-warranty-residential-commercial-standby-generators.pdf)). The activation code can be obtained by the owner by web or phone ([manual](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/Generac_9kw-22kw_Installation_Manual.pdf)).
- **Kohler is stricter:** coverage "begins on the commissioning date if registered by your dealer, or defaults to the shipment date" ([Kohler](https://www.kohlerhomeenergy.rehlko.com/owner-support)), and manuals require an authorized distributor/dealer to install ([TP-7101](https://resources.kohler.com/power/kohler/residential/pdf/tp7101.pdf)).
- **Pad choice is more flexible than marketing suggests.** Generac permits "pea gravel, compacted soil, crushed stone, or a concrete pad," requires level within 0.5 in, and defers to local code ([manual](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/Generac_9kw-22kw_Installation_Manual.pdf)). Cummins specifies a 3 in slab at least 2 in larger than the base ([quick start](https://www.manualshelf.com/manual/cummins-power-generation/a065d231/quick-start-installer-english.html)).
- **Storms produce measurable install backlogs.** Post-Helene, one NC county's generator permit requests went from 17 in three months to 177 over eight months, with dealers booked 4 weeks to 4 months out ([The Mountaineer](https://www.themountaineer.com/news/never-again-generator-sales-spike-after-helene-power-outages/article_02179a0f-8f63-4f6c-85e1-d0081ad815a5.html)).
- **Post-storm gouging on generators is documented and prosecuted.** South Carolina logged 415+ Helene complaints naming generators, Georgia 287; NC fines reach $5,000 per violation, Florida $1,000 up to $25,000 in 24 hours ([More Perfect Union](https://substack.perfectunion.us/p/helene-and-milton-show-the-reality); [Florida AG](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/consumer-protection/price-gouging)). In Florida, unlicensed contracting during a declared emergency is a third-degree felony ([DBPR](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hurricane-guide/)).
- **NEC 702.4 separates good installers from bad.** With an ATS, the source must supply the full calculated load per Article 220 *or* use an energy management system to shed load ([NC OSFM](https://www.ncosfm.gov/electrical/articles-702-705-706-710-optional-standby-systems-stand-alone-systems-energy-storage-systems/open)).

## Detailed findings

### 1. The full sequence, phase by phase

| # | Phase | Typical 2025–26 duration | Who performs it | Notes / delay risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Initial consult / in-home assessment | 1–3 days to schedule; 1–2 hrs onsite | Dealer salesperson, ideally with the electrician | Scheduling stretches in post-storm markets |
| 2 | Load calculation | Same visit to 3 days | Licensed electrician | NEC 702.4 / Art. 220; skipped by weak installers |
| 3 | Site survey: placement, gas run, panel, setbacks | Same visit | Dealer + gas fitter | Should include a gas load check against meter capacity |
| 4 | Written itemized quote | 1–3 days | Dealer | Should line-item generator, ATS, pad, gas, electrical, permits, startup |
| 5 | Contract + deposit | Homeowner-paced | — | — |
| 6 | Permit application (electrical + gas/mechanical, sometimes building/zoning) | 3–10 business days typical; 2–4 weeks in slower AHJs | Contractor | ([iBLighting](https://iblighting.com/standby-generator-installation-cost/); [PermitMint](https://permitmint.com/guides/generator/)) |
| 7 | HOA / historic district approval | 0–4+ weeks | Homeowner + dealer | Runs in parallel; a real delay in covenanted subdivisions |
| 8 | Gas meter / service upgrade (if triggered) | 2–4 weeks typical; 4–8 weeks in some utilities | **Gas utility only** | The most commonly missed schedule risk |
| 9 | Electrical panel upgrade (if triggered) | 2–3 weeks | Electrician | Adds $2,800–$5,000 in NJ pricing ([Malfettone](https://malfettoneelectric.com/blog/generator-installation-timeline-nj)) |
| 10 | Equipment order / delivery | Days if in dealer stock; 1–2 weeks if ordered | Dealer | Not a 2026 constraint absent a storm |
| 11 | Pad prep / site prep | 0.5–1 day (plus 3–7 days cure if poured concrete) | Dealer crew, or excavator/concrete sub | Composite pads eliminate cure time |
| 12 | Set the unit | Hours | Dealer crew; crane for larger units | — |
| 13 | Gas line run, pressure test, connection | 1–2 days for a 50–75 ft run | Licensed plumber / gas fitter | Pressure test per IFGC §406 |
| 14 | Electrical rough-in, ATS, panel work | Hours to 1 day; main power off 2–4 hrs | Licensed electrician | — |
| 15 | Inspections (rough and/or final, electrical + gas) | 1–5 days after install; up to 1–2 weeks | AHJ | Scheduling, not the inspection, is the delay |
| 16 | Startup / commissioning + activation | 1–3 hrs, often same day as install | Dealer technician | Load test, gas pressure under load, transfer test |
| 17 | Warranty registration | Same day | Dealer (recommended) | Sets the coverage start date |
| 18 | Homeowner walkthrough / training | 30–60 min | Dealer technician | Exercise schedule, monitoring app, manual transfer |

**End to end: 4–8 weeks is the honest range for a straightforward job with no utility or panel upgrade.** Add 4–8 weeks if the gas service must be upgraded. Add weeks to months in a post-storm surge market.

### 2. Equipment lead times: verify, don't repeat 2021 talking points

**The durable rule: for residential air-cooled units, the equipment is almost never the bottleneck.
Permits, the gas utility, and installer labor are.** The 2021–2022 pandemic backlog is over;
manufacturers added plant automation specifically so that storm surges no longer translate into
factory backlogs. Any content telling homeowners to expect a six-to-twelve-month wait on the machine
itself is repeating pandemic-era talking points.

**Two traps when checking current conditions.** First, the very long lead times that circulate in
trade press — figures in the forty-week range — are for **large-megawatt data center gensets**, not
home standby. Do not conflate the two; it is the single most common error in this area. Second,
manufacturer commentary describes national conditions, and local reality can diverge sharply from it
in a storm market.

**Storms still spike everything, and the constraint is local labor rather than factory output.** A
dealer sitting on a full yard of inventory that is entirely pre-sold is the characteristic pattern
after a regional event: units are available, crews to install them are not. Manufacturers respond to
major storms with meaningful hiring, which tells you the surge is real but also that it is absorbed
over months rather than weeks.

*Current-quarter shipment, revenue, and dealer-count figures are deliberately excluded here; see
`08-outage-data-and-market.md`.*

### 3. Who does what

Three trades are always involved, whether one company employs them all or not:

- **Licensed electrician** — load calc, feeder from generator to ATS (sized at 115% of nameplate per NEC 445.13), ATS install, panel integration, grounding/bonding, permit. Generac's manual: "Only a trained and licensed electrician should perform wiring and connections to unit" ([Generac EcoGen manual](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/Generac_15kw_Ecogen_Installation_Manual.pdf)).
- **Licensed plumber / gas fitter** — pipe sizing, run, pressure test, sediment trap, flexible connector. Generac: "Gas line connections should be made by a certified plumber familiar with local codes."
- **Dealer crew** — pad, setting the unit, controller config, startup, activation, registration, walkthrough.
- **Excavator or GC** — only when trenching is long, the pad requires significant grading, retaining/drainage work is needed, or the unit must be elevated above a flood elevation.

**Why the buyer should care.** A self-performing company puts one entity on the hook for the whole job; a coordination model can produce finger-pointing between trades when something fails ([ITDG](https://itdgconstruction.com/blog/self-performing-contractor-vs-subcontracting)). Big-box retail programs generally sell the unit and dispatch third-party local crews — often good crews, but the homeowner doesn't pick which one, and post-install service may route elsewhere ([Complete Power Resources](https://jacksonville.completepowerresources.com/blog/home-depot-vs-local-installer-jacksonville) — installer marketing, directionally useful). The question to hand a buyer: *"Which parts do you self-perform, and who specifically holds the electrical license on my job?"* ([Pro-Tech-Power](https://www.ptpnw.com/2026/05/20/whole-home-generator-installers-questions/)).

### 4. Pad options

Generac's installation manual is permissive: prepare a rectangular area ~5 in deep and ~6 in longer/wider than the footprint, compacted and level; "pea gravel, compacted soil, crushed stone, or a concrete pad" all appear, followed by "check local codes to see what type is required." The unit must be **level within 0.5 in** all around ([Generac 9–22 kW manual](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/Generac_9kw-22kw_Installation_Manual.pdf)).

- **Composite/polymer pad (e.g., Generac Composite GENPAD):** 33 lbs, 1,200 lb load rating, UL 94-5V and NFPA 37 fire rated, UL746C UV rated, warranted for the generator's warranty period; Generac claims "shorter installation time compared to pea gravel, poured concrete, or concrete pads" ([spec sheet](https://www.generac.com/globalassets/products/residential/standby-generators/spec-sheets/composite-genpad-a0003728171-spec-sheet.pdf)). Fits air-cooled 10–26 kW on stable, well-drained, compacted subgrade. No cure time.
- **Poured concrete:** for jurisdictions that require it, units elevated above base flood elevation, hurricane wind-anchoring zones, soft or expansive soils, and liquid-cooled units. Some jurisdictions specify ~4 in thick extending ~2 in above grade on compacted fill ([PermitMint](https://permitmint.com/guides/generator/) — aggregator, verify locally).
- **Gravel bed:** permitted by Generac and cheapest, but hardest to keep level and most prone to erosion; Generac requires a base that "will not erode over time."

**Frost and settling.** Neither Generac nor Cummins requires a frost-depth footing for an air-cooled residential unit — these pads sit on grade. The frost-depth rule (IRC R403.1.4, 12 in minimum below undisturbed grade, deeper up north) governs structural footings ([IRC 2021 summary](https://concretetoolkit.com/calculators/concrete-footing-depth-calculator-usa/)). The practical concern is differential settling: a tilting pad loads the flexible fuel connector and increases vibration ([Grounded Electric](https://www.grounded-electric.com/how-to-hook-up-a-generac-generator/)). Slope grade away from the pad; use flexible conduit to absorb seasonal movement.

### 5. Placement decisions

- **Clearances (Generac, air-cooled):** 3 ft front and ends including shrubs and trees; 18 in rear; 5 ft overhead from any structure, overhang, or projection; no installation under a wooden deck without 5 ft overhead ([instruction sheet](https://productmanuals.generac.com/api/manualfiles/G0072580/A0005151099/0)).
- **Openings:** NFPA 37 §4.1.4 defaults to 5 ft from wall openings and from structures with combustible walls. The 18-in rear reduction comes from full-scale SwRI fire testing and **does not apply to openings** ([Generac install guide](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/10kw-28kw_install_guide.pdf)). This Old House states the field rule as 5 ft from combustibles like wood siding, 3 ft from noncombustibles like brick ([This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/electrical/backup-power)).
- **Distance to panel and meter:** shorter runs are cheaper and reduce pressure-drop risk. Generac: "Install the generator as close as possible to the fuel supply to reduce the length of piping" ([EcoGen manual](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/Generac_15kw_Ecogen_Installation_Manual.pdf)). Convenience never overrides the opening/CO setback.
- **Exhaust and CO:** exhaust must not aim at windows, doors, soffit vents, or window wells. Kohler requires CO detectors on each level of any adjacent building ([8/10/12RESV](https://resources.kohler.com/power/kohler/residential/pdf/8RESVL_INSTALLATION.pdf)).
- **Noise:** a Generac 16–22 kW Guardian publishes 67 dB(A) at 23 ft under normal load and 55–57 dB(A) in Quiet-Test weekly exercise, programmable weekly/bi-weekly/monthly for 5–20 minutes ([spec sheet](https://www.generac.com/globalassets/products/residential/standby-generators/spec-sheets/16-22kw-guardian-standby-generator-specsheet.pdf)). Site away from bedroom windows — yours and the neighbor's — and check municipal setback and noise ordinances, which vary widely.
- **Drainage and flooding:** Generac calls for special attention to piping strength in flood, hurricane, tornado, earthquake, and unstable-ground areas. Cummins requires the service regulator be installed **above the expected snow line**. In flood zones the pad must sit above base flood elevation.

### 6. Commissioning and startup

Commissioning is where a good install is proven. Generac's documented startup sequence includes verifying fuel lines were purged and leak-tested per fuel-gas code, recording **static** gas pressure with a manometer, recording **loaded** pressure at full load, and checking transfer switch operation. The manual warns that if loaded pressure falls below spec "the generator may not function properly," and that a fluctuating needle "indicates that gas piping may be undersized or restricted" ([Generac 9–22 kW manual](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/Generac_9kw-22kw_Installation_Manual.pdf)).

**Activation vs. warranty — get this right.** Generac units will not run in AUTO until an activation code is entered, and the manual describes the *owner* obtaining that code via generac.com or 1-888-9ACTIVATE. "Only a factory dealer can activate your Generac" is therefore **not accurate as a manufacturer requirement**. What is: warranty "begins upon the successful start-up and/or on-line activation," the unit must be registered with proof of purchase, and all warranty repairs must go through an Independent Authorized Service Dealer ([Generac 5-year terms](https://www.generac.com/globalassets/products/residential/standby-generators/warranty/5-year-limited-warranty-residential-commercial-standby-generators.pdf)).

Kohler and Cummins are stricter on paper. Kohler requires an authorized distributor/dealer to install, completion of startup and installation checklists plus a signed startup notification form, and registration through its online Warranty Processing System; coverage "begins on the commissioning date if registered by your dealer, or defaults to the shipment date" ([TP-7101](https://resources.kohler.com/power/kohler/residential/pdf/tp7101.pdf); [Kohler](https://www.kohlerhomeenergy.rehlko.com/owner-support)). Cummins requires a certified electrician and dealer-handled registration ([Cummins FAQ](https://www.cummins.com/na/generators/home-standby/home-generator-faq)).

### 7. Seasonality, storm surges, and price gouging

Manufacturers do not forecast assuming a major hurricane or winter storm; when one hits, sales spike immediately. The concrete off-season argument is the local install queue, and Hurricane Helene left a clean illustration of it: permit requests in one western North Carolina county went from 17 in the three months before the storm to 177 over the following eight months, with dealers quoting anywhere from four weeks to four months and one operator summarizing it as "the longer they put it off, the farther out it's putting them" ([The Mountaineer, June 2025](https://www.themountaineer.com/news/never-again-generator-sales-spike-after-helene-power-outages/article_02179a0f-8f63-4f6c-85e1-d0081ad815a5.html)). Cite this as a documented case study of what a storm does to a local queue, not as a current condition.

**Price gouging.** Thirty-nine states plus DC and several territories have price-gouging statutes tied to declared emergencies ([NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/financial-services/price-gouging-state-statutes)). Florida compares the charged price to the 30-day pre-emergency average and looks for "gross disparity," with penalties of $1,000 per violation up to $25,000 in 24 hours ([Florida AG](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/consumer-protection/price-gouging)). Texas names generators among protected necessities once the governor declares a disaster ([Texas AG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/ag-paxton-warns-texans-price-gouging-wake-hurricane-hanna)). Post-Helene complaint counts: South Carolina 415+, Georgia 287, North Carolina 64+, Florida 200+ ([More Perfect Union](https://substack.perfectunion.us/p/helene-and-milton-show-the-reality); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/price-gouging-complaints-surge-hurricane-helene-states-fuel-water-hotels-2024-10)).

**Storm chasers.** In Florida, unlicensed construction during a declared state of emergency is a third-degree felony and DBPR runs door-to-door sweeps with law enforcement ([DBPR](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hurricane-guide/)); the Hillsborough State Attorney publicized exactly this after Helene and Milton ([FOX 13](https://www.fox13news.com/news/beware-unlicensed-contractors-trying-con-bay-area-residents-repairing-homes-after-recent-hurricanes)). North Carolina's contractor licensing board issues the same warning annually ([NCLBGC, May 2025](https://www.nclbgc.org/2025/06/03/after-the-storm-comes-the-scam-protect-yourself/)).

### 8. Common mistakes and callbacks, ranked by how often sources cite them

1. **Undersized gas pipe.** Named as the most common mistake by Generac itself. Symptom: starts fine, then derates or shuts down minutes into a real outage. Contractors on the Mike Holt forum describe gensets "falling on their face at 50% load because of low pressure/insufficient BTU input" (anecdotal, practitioner forum — [Mike Holt](https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/home-electrical-generators-gas-meters-and-regulators.2573076/)).
2. **Meter/service capacity ignored.** The pipe run can be perfect and still starve the unit if the meter is undersized for house plus generator ([Grounded Electric](https://www.grounded-electric.com/do-i-need-a-to-upgrade-my-gas-meter-for-a-generator/)).
3. **No real load calculation.** NEC 702.4(A)(2) requires full calculated load per Article 220 or an EMS load-shed scheme; quoting kW off a rule of thumb is the shortcut ([NC OSFM](https://www.ncosfm.gov/electrical/articles-702-705-706-710-optional-standby-systems-stand-alone-systems-energy-storage-systems/open); [EC&M](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/qa/article/20898908/code-qa-sizing-an-optional-standby-generator)).
4. **Wrong ATS type, or wrong bonding for that type.** A service-entrance-rated ATS becomes the service disconnect and the main panel becomes a subpanel — the bond moves and the main panel's neutral bar must be isolated. Non-service-rated: the bond stays at the main panel; never add a second ([ATS guide](https://www.onesto-ep.com/blog/7-steps-to-wire-an-automatic-transfer-switch-for-generators/) — contractor blog, verify against NEC 250.30).
5. **Improper neutral bonding at the generator.** Generac: bonding inside the generator applies only to separately derived systems; "it is not required when the generator is a backup source in a utility supplied electrical system with a 2-pole transfer switch" ([manual](https://apelectric.com/content/PDF/Generac/Generac_9kw-22kw_Installation_Manual.pdf)).
6. **Clearance and opening violations** — too close to a window, under a deck without 5 ft overhead, shrubs grown into the intake.
7. **Exhaust aimed at an opening**, even where spacing technically complies.
8. **Skipped permits** — frequently flagged as the line item homeowners cut ([iBLighting](https://iblighting.com/standby-generator-installation-cost/), installer blog).
9. **Poor drainage or a settling pad**, which stresses the flexible fuel connector.

### 9. What the homeowner decides, and what a bad installer skips

**Homeowner decisions:** whole-house vs. essential circuits (this drives ATS type and generator size); natural gas vs. LP (LP needs a tank, 250 gal minimum per Generac); location, trading noise and neighbor sightlines against the cost of longer runs; pad type where code allows a choice; exercise day and time; monitoring and maintenance plans.

**What a good installer does that a bad one skips:** a real site visit before quoting; a written load calculation; a gas load check against meter capacity *before* permits are filed; an itemized quote separating generator, ATS, pad, gas, electrical, permits, trenching, startup, and exclusions; pulling permits in their own name; a full-load commissioning test with a manometer on the gas line; registering the warranty and handing over the confirmation; and a walkthrough covering manual transfer, exercise schedule, and the break-in oil change. Red flags: phone quotes with no site visit, suggesting permits be skipped, refusing to line-item ([Complete Power Resources](https://jacksonville.completepowerresources.com/blog/home-depot-vs-local-installer-jacksonville); [TLC Electrical](https://tlcelectrical.com/generac-installer-in-dfw-questions/)).

## Claims to avoid

- **"Generator lead times are 6–12 months."** Pandemic-era, and unsupported for residential air-cooled units. The much longer lead-time figures that circulate in trade press are for data center gensets, not home standby.
- **Any current-conditions claim about lead times, backlogs, or dealer capacity.** These change by quarter and by market. Write about what governs the timeline — permits, gas utility coordination, installer labor — rather than what this quarter looks like.
- **"Only a factory-authorized dealer can activate your Generac."** The manual documents the owner obtaining the code by web or phone. What's true: warranty *repairs* must go through an Independent Authorized Service Dealer.
- **"Installation by a non-dealer voids your warranty."** Too blunt and brand-dependent. State the brand-specific rule.
- **"You must have a poured concrete pad."** Generac permits gravel, crushed stone, and compacted soil. Say local code may require concrete.
- **"Generators must be 5 feet from the house."** Conflates the NFPA 37 opening setback with the wall setback; Generac allows 18 inches at the rear under the SwRI exception.
- **National dollar figures for permits, meter upgrades, or panel upgrades.** The ranges found ($100–$500 permits, $2,800–$5,000 panel upgrade) are single-market installer blogs.
- **"Unpermitted generators void your homeowners insurance."** Asserted by installer blogs; no carrier or regulatory source found.
- **Any national percentage of installs failing inspection, or share of installers who subcontract.** No data exists.
